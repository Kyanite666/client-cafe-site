Our Story

It began with a secondhand oven and a lot of stubbornness. Sisters Maggie and Claire Hendry had spent years baking for friends, neighbours, and anyone who happened to wander through their family kitchen. Maggie's blueberry muffins became a fixture at school fundraisers, and Claire's chocolate cake showed up at every birthday on the street. Eventually someone told them they should be selling these. After a lot of late nights, a small loan, and one very questionable paint colour, they did. The building at 123 Main Street had been empty for almost two years. It used to be a hardware store, and the sisters kept the original timber floors and the long wooden counter, which still carries the scratches of a hundred years of customers. On opening day they baked forty muffins and expected to take home most of them. They sold out by 9:30 am. Word spread quickly, the way it does in a small town. People came for the coffee and stayed for the croissants, and the flat whites with fern-patterned latte art became the order of choice for the morning regulars. Over the years the menu grew to include fresh garden salads, iced lattes for the long summer afternoons, and the egg croissant that now has a loyal following of its own. More than a decade on, the recipe is still the same: bake everything fresh, brew every cup with care, and treat every customer like someone you've known for years. The original oven is still here too, and still working hard. We'd love to see you at 123 Main Street.
